
The case comes with a dossier: standard checks already run for this candidate, with evidence ids and images. Work as a careful Holter reader. Principle of this procedure: every explanation, ischemic or not, must earn its place through its own positive signature in this candidate's measurements. The absence of one explanation's signature is not evidence for another, and a label is never chosen just because nothing better fits.

Step 1. Readable? From signal_quality, rhythm_context (including rr_irregularity), QRS width and eligibility_check decide whether the ST segment can be interpreted. If it cannot, the label is indeterminate_data and you stop here.

Step 2. Size the real change (episode_context): per involved lead, the extremum minus the local level before the change, and whether the candidate is a fragment of a longer change or the return between two episodes. Use this local change, not the deviation from the record-wide reference, in the steps below.

Step 3. Check the signatures. For each element write present, absent or unclear, with its evidence id. When an element that could change the result is unclear and one tool call can settle it, make that call: compare_morphology against the same lead just before onset (QRS change), measure_window just before onset and at the extremum (shape, and the ST level at a similar heart rate), st_trend around the episode (time course), view_ecg at the onset (step, artifact). Skip calls that cannot change an element.

Ischemia signature (count the elements present, 0 to 4):
 I1 Shape at the extremum. Read j20_uv to j60_uv (slope_mv_per_s runs from the J point, so a depressed J point that recovers gives a positive slope even when the segment after it is flat). Present: horizontal or downsloping depression; a transient elevation (vasospastic attacks show "ST elevation in leads corresponding to the culprit lesion", K28); or an upsloping depression that is still about 200 uV or deeper at the ST point (junctional depression over 2 mm with a slowly ascending segment carried the same rate of coronary disease as horizontal depression, K24). Absent: the heart-rate related shapes listed in the Reference knowledge, or an upsloping depression that is shallow by the ST point (upsloping depression was less often ischemic than horizontal or downsloping, K25). A T-wave change on its own is not I1 (K37).
 I2 Time course. Present: gradual onset and offset over a minute or more (ACC/AHA criterion in the Reference knowledge), or a slow build-up over many minutes from a small lead-in (ischemic deviation "developed gradually, over 15 to 20 minutes", K16). Absent: most of the change happens within one 15-s step (episode_dynamics max_single_step_frac, onset_to_90pct_s).
 I3 Heart-rate relation. Present if one holds: (a) the change appears at a normal resting heart rate without a rise of 10 bpm, while the same lead at a similar heart rate just before onset is near its local level ("ST-segment shifts at normal heart rate" support spasm-related ischemia, K29); (b) heart rate peaks before the ST extremum and ST stays deviated while heart rate is already falling back (peak heart rate preceded maximal depression by about 80 s and heart rate was often back at baseline while ST was still depressed, K01; depression continuing while heart rate falls, K02); (c) ST stays level over lower heart rates and then falls steeply above a knee (K02). A lag of only a few tens of seconds is within the tools' resolution and does not count.
 I4 Depth. Present: horizontal or downsloping depression deeper than about 150 to 200 uV from the local level, or an elevation of 150 uV or more (in healthy older men, Holter specificity was 1.0 for horizontal or downsloping depression over 0.20 mV or elevation of 0.15 mV or more, K26). Absent: a local change near 100 uV or less.

Alternative signatures (for the first three, count the two elements: "partial" with one, "full" with both):
 Heart-rate related. H1: heart rate actually changed, a rise of 10 bpm or more before or during the candidate (the size Panza et al. used, K03; episode_dynamics hr block). H2: ST follows heart rate up and down closely and recovers as heart rate recovers, with an ST change per bpm within the patient's outside-episode range (rate_response), or the heart-rate related shapes of the Reference knowledge at the extremum, or horizontal or downsloping depression appearing only at sinus rates around 130 bpm or more (seen in healthy volunteers "usually in association with tachycardia (135 +/- 10.5 beats/min)", K10). H1 is the precondition: without H1 this explanation is not available, whatever the shape, because a heart-rate related change needs a heart-rate change; H1 alone counts as none (see Step 4); H1 with H2 only partly shown (ST tracks heart rate but the per-bpm comparison or the shape is unclear) is partial; H1 with H2 is full.
 Early repolarization (elevation only). E1: the elevation is present when heart rate is slow and disappears when heart rate rises ("seen only below heart rate (HR) of about 70/min", K32). E2: outside ST episodes the same lead is usually elevated at that slow heart rate too (rate_response st_by_heart_rate_band: outside-episode median at the candidate's heart-rate band close to the candidate's level). If the same lead is near its local level at the same slow heart rate just before onset, or the outside-episode median at that band is near zero, both are absent.
 Position or axis. P1: a Q-, R- or S-wave amplitude or shape change in the same lead at the same moment as the ST change (compare_morphology against the beat just before onset; morphology_steps synchronous_st_and_qrs_steps), in a lead whose R wave is large enough to judge (the ACC/AHA guideline asks for R waves of 10 mm or more, K39; eligibility flag leads_r_wave_below_1mv). P2: abrupt step-like onset or offset with little heart-rate change or motion artifact (positional changes have "a very sudden onset and offset associated with motion artifact with no or little change in heart rate", K14). A QRS change that grows and recedes gradually with a large ST change, mostly in the lead with the largest ST deviation, is not P1: transient ischemia can itself raise R-wave voltage and shift the axis (K18) and lengthen the QRS with higher terminal voltage (K19).
 The next three are single signatures: full when clearly present, partial when only partly so.
 Conduction: QRS widening with changed complexes, appearing and disappearing with the ST change.
 Noise: the extremum lies in a noisy, saturated or flat stretch, or the change is made of non-normal or irregular beats.
 Reference or fragment (other_nonischemic): Step 2 found that most of the deviation was already present before the change (local change much smaller than the deviation from the reference), or that the candidate is a fragment of, or the return between, longer changes.

Step 4. Not signatures; never list these as findings for or against: good signal quality; the absence of a QRS step or of noise; fixed properties of the record shared by all its candidates (number or names of leads, a lead's usual ST level, R-wave size: these only limit how far a QRS comparison in that lead can be trusted); items eligibility_check cannot assess; the ACC/AHA depression check for an elevation candidate; recurrence of similar episodes in the record, or similarity to the patient's other episodes ("in the same individual, various episodes may be due to different mechanisms", K43); a heart-rate rise on its own (89% of daily-life ischemic episodes followed a rise of at least 10 bpm, K03).

Step 5. Tools can be wrong: before relying on an element that decides the row or column below, check its number with a second view (another tool, lead, time or the image).

Step 6. Turn the signatures into p_ischemic. Take the alternative with the most elements present (A: none = no element of any alternative; partial = one element; full = both elements) and the ischemia count (I = 0 to 4), and start from this band:
   A none:    I 0: 0.45-0.55 | I 1: 0.58-0.66 | I 2: 0.68-0.76 | I 3-4: 0.80-0.90
   A partial: I 0: 0.30-0.40 | I 1: 0.42-0.50 | I 2: 0.52-0.60 | I 3-4: 0.62-0.72
   A full:    I 0: 0.08-0.18 | I 1: 0.20-0.30 | I 2: 0.32-0.42 | I 3-4: 0.44-0.54
   Within the band go higher when the local change is large and seen in more than one lead, lower when the elements rest on one unclear number. The label must agree: ischemic if p_ischemic >= 0.5, otherwise the alternative with the most elements present (other_nonischemic if none has any).

Step 7. Submit with submit_assessment: p_ischemic; the label; findings_for_ischemia, one entry per ischemia element present ("I1: ...", "I3b: ..."); findings_against_ischemia, one entry per alternative element present ("H1: ...", "P2: ..."), each with its evidence ids; and a rationale that states the ischemia count, the strongest alternative with its elements, and the band. Use indeterminate_data only when the signal does not allow ST interpretation; when the evidence conflicts, let the probability show it instead of abstaining.
Then reply with a one-paragraph summary.
