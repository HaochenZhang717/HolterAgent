
The case comes with a dossier: standard checks already run for this candidate, with evidence ids and images. Work as a careful Holter reader and follow these steps in order.

Step 1. Can the ST segment be read? Check signal_quality, rhythm_context (including rr_irregularity), QRS width and the images. If it cannot be read, the label is indeterminate_data. Otherwise go on.

Step 2. Fill the checklist. For each item write one verdict: FOR ischemia, AGAINST ischemia, or NEUTRAL (not shown, contradictory, or not measurable), with the evidence id. Read the dossier first; call a tool only when an item is still unclear and the tool named there would settle it.
- A. Real local change (episode_context). FOR: a clear change from the local level just before it (extremum minus local level) that starts and ends around the candidate. AGAINST: the candidate is mostly a fragment or tail of a longer change, the return between two episodes, or a lead sitting at its usual offset, so the change from the local level is small. If unclear: st_trend over the surrounding 20 to 30 minutes.
- B. Time course (episode_dynamics; morphology_steps st_steps). FOR: gradual onset and offset over a minute or more (see the ACC/AHA definition in Context). AGAINST: a step-like jump (most of the change within one 15-s step, or an ST step listed by morphology_steps) followed by a flat new level. If unclear: view_ecg or measure_window just before and just after the onset.
- C. Shape at the extremum compared with the same lead before the change (measure_window J+20 to J+60 and the image; compare_morphology). FOR: horizontal or downsloping depression, scooping, or an elevation that rises and falls smoothly (the ischemic shapes in Context). AGAINST: J-point depression with a steep positive slope whose ST point lies near the lead's usual level, the T wave moving into the ST segment, T-wave peaking, or a parallel shift (the heart-rate related shapes in Context). If unclear: measure_window before the onset and compare_morphology against it.
- D. ST at matched heart rate (rate_response st_by_heart_rate_band and uv_per_bpm; episode_dynamics hr). Apply the Heart rate guidance in Context: FOR when the ST at this heart rate, or the ST change per bpm, is far beyond this patient's usual; AGAINST only when it is within the patient's usual range and the ST change follows the heart rate up and down. A heart-rate rise before onset is NEUTRAL by itself (K03).
- E. Position or axis (morphology_steps synchronous_st_and_qrs_steps and qrs_steps; compare_morphology qrs_corr, qrs_pp_ratio, polarity_flip). AGAINST: a QRS amplitude or shape step at the same time as the ST change, in the same lead, with an abrupt ST change. FOR (weak): the QRS stays stable while the ST changes gradually. NEUTRAL: a QRS change only in a lead with a small QRS (eligibility_check leads_r_wave_below_1mv, small qrs_pp_ref_uv; the guideline asks for R waves of 10 mm or more, K39), or a QRS change that grows and recedes gradually with the ST change (see Position and conduction in Context).
- F. Conduction (qrs_ms against qrs_ms_record_median; beat labels). AGAINST: the QRS widens or changes to bizarre beats at the onset. NEUTRAL: QRS width unchanged.
- G. Noise (signal_quality in the episode; view_ecg). AGAINST: the deviation comes with noise, saturation or baseline wander in the same lead. NEUTRAL: clean signal (it makes the ST readable; it is not evidence for or against ischemia).

Step 3. What is NOT evidence. Do not list these as findings for or against ischemia; mention them in the rationale only as limits: good signal quality; the number or names of the leads and the lack of contiguous 12-lead groups; that a criterion "cannot be excluded"; that the ACC/AHA depression rule does not apply to an elevation; that the record has many similar episodes (in one person separate episodes may arise from different mechanisms, K43; judge this candidate on items A to G).

Step 4. Set p_ischemic from the checklist: take the first band whose condition holds, then place the value within the band by the strength of the items. Items B, C and D describe the change itself; E, F and G are the alternative causes. Treat A NEUTRAL like A FOR and stay in the lower half of the band.
- 0.85 to 0.95: A FOR, at least two of B, C, D FOR and none AGAINST, and none of E, F, G AGAINST.
- 0.65 to 0.85: A FOR, more of B, C, D FOR than AGAINST, and none of E, F, G AGAINST.
- 0.45 to 0.65: A FOR, and either B, C, D are balanced (as many FOR as AGAINST, or all NEUTRAL) with none of E, F, G AGAINST, or two or more of B, C, D are FOR while one of E, F, G is AGAINST.
- 0.25 to 0.45: A FOR with more of B, C, D AGAINST than FOR (the change itself looks heart-rate related) and none of E, F, G AGAINST; or A AGAINST with at least one of B, C, D FOR; or one of E, F, G AGAINST with exactly one of B, C, D FOR.
- 0.05 to 0.25: one of E, F, G AGAINST and none of B, C, D FOR; or A AGAINST and none of B, C, D FOR.
The label must agree with p_ischemic: ischemic if p_ischemic >= 0.5, otherwise the non-ischemic cause whose item was AGAINST (A: other_nonischemic; B or C or D: heart_rate_related or axis_or_position_shift, whichever fits; E: axis_or_position_shift; F: conduction_change; G: noise_artifact).

Step 5. Submit with submit_assessment: p_ischemic; the label; findings_for_ischemia and findings_against_ischemia taken from the items with FOR and AGAINST verdicts (one finding per item, with its evidence id; NEUTRAL items go in neither list); and a rationale that begins with the seven verdicts in one line, for example "A FOR (E12), B FOR (E15), C NEUTRAL, D FOR (E18), E NEUTRAL, F FOR (E11), G NEUTRAL", followed by two or three sentences. Use indeterminate_data only when the signal does not allow ST interpretation; when the evidence conflicts, let the probability show it instead of abstaining.
Then reply with a one-paragraph summary.
